## What it shows

- **Bifrost v2.6.0 requires a virtual key.** Every call got 401 with `virtual key is required. Provide a virtual key
  via the x-bf-vk header.` No call reached Prefixwise, so Bifrost is still untested. Its config schema has
  `client.enforce_auth_on_inference` and `governance.virtual_keys`. Round 3 sets a virtual key.
- **Portkey's streaming failure is Portkey's own.** The control streamed straight to the cache mock, with no
  Prefixwise in the path, and got the same 500 on every call. Its log shows the same `TypeError: immutable` in
  `updateHeaders`. Through Prefixwise, every streamed call was recorded and every change named, in both formats.
  Whether Portkey 1.15.2 streams from a real provider is untested.
- **Plano exposed a bug in Prefixwise: chunked request bodies were read as empty.**
  - Every call was recorded, with the conversation id and without errors, but every turn read `0/0` tokens.
  - Every turn after the first was `unknown` on OpenAI and `breakpoint_misplaced` on Anthropic.
  - `prefixwise serve` read a request body only by its `Content-Length`. A body sent with
    `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` and no length was read as empty, and an empty body was forwarded upstream. The
    cache mock answers an empty body with 0 prompt tokens. A real provider would have rejected it.
  - The checker's new `--chunked` option sends bodies this way. On the code that ran here, it gives exactly Plano's
    two patterns: `first_write` then `unknown` four times on OpenAI, and `breakpoint_misplaced` four times on
    Anthropic.
  - That Plano sends its bodies chunked was not captured directly. These matching results are the evidence, and
    round 3 runs Plano again on the fixed code.
  - The fix reads chunked bodies whole (see the CHANGELOG). With it, the `--chunked` self-check gives 50 of 50
    recorded and 10 of 10 right, streamed and not.
